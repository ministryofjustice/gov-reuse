# Example Article

Hook the reader. Be bold. This is an introduction to the article.
We can use long and short sentences, to build a rythm, and breath life into an otherwise dry subject.
Remember to provide context to the article. Avoid cleche phrases like "let's jump in" – you are not ChatGPT.
Let your pashion for the subject shine through. Use a conversational tone, and write in a way that is engaging and easy to read.

Now if the article poses a question, give the user a tldr answer upfront.
Maybe it's a definition, a table of data, a video, or a simple list.
Don't make the reader scroll through the whole article to get the answer.
The rest of the article is to provide depth to the topic and support the answer.

## Article Structure

Each sub topic should be clearly defined by its heading, and should be written in a way that is easy to read and understand.
It should stand on its own, if it was read in isolation from the rest of the article.

Every sub topic you write about should be "on topic", in other words, it should be relevant to the main topic of the article.
For example, if we're writing about "How to leverage AI in UX design", a sub topic about "Using AI to ideate microcopy"
could fit in well. But talking about training AI models, or technical details of how AI generates images, would be off-topic.

If you are in any doubt, about the article structure, it's worth reading other articles on the subject.
This is not to plagiarise, but to see what headings and topics they use, so you can get a structure for your content
and ensure you are meeting the goal of topic completeness.

## The Article's Topic Coverage & Depth

Most topics can be broken down into 3-5 sub topics, but this is not a hard rule.
Word count doesn't matter. The most important thing is to cover the topic fully.
Depending on complexity, sub topics will vary in length, but a good rule of thumb is 2-5 paragraphs per sub topic.

## Using Formatting To Enhance Readability

We write articles in Markdown format, which is a lightweight markup language that allows us to format text easily.
We can use headings, lists, links, images, and other formatting options to make the article more engaging and easier to read.
We can also use code blocks for any code snippets, and tables for any data that needs to be presented in a structured way.

Here are some of the most common formatting options we can use in Markdown:

- **Headings:** Use `#` for headings. The number of `#` symbols indicates the level of the heading.
- **Bold:** Surround words with `**` or `__` to make text bold.
- **Italic:** Surround words with `*` or `_` to make text italic.
- **Lists:** Use `-` or `*` for unordered lists, and numbers for ordered lists.
- **Links:** Use `[text](url)` to create a hyperlink.
- **Code blocks:** Use triple backticks ``` for code blocks.
- **Blockquotes:** Use `>` for blockquotes.
- **Horizontal rules:** Use `---` or `***` for horizontal rules.

For more information on Markdown formatting, see the [Markdown Guide](https://www.markdownguide.org/).

## Summary

When writing an article for Reuse Library, focus on providing value to the reader by being clear, concise, and engaging.
Organise information in the article into a clear structure with sub topics that are relevant to the main topic.
Use [Markdown](https://www.markdownguide.org/) formatting options to make the article easy to read and understand.
Don't focus on word count, but rather on providing depth and completeness to the topic.
Most of all remember to have fun writing, and remember you can reach out with questions on the #reuse-library channel in Slack.
